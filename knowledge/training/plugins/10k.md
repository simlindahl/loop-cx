# LOOP 10K performance adaptation

**Generic translated reference draft · not an individualized plan or delivered app feature**

## Purpose and entry

This optional time-limited adaptation emphasizes a road 10K while retaining the [LOOP system](../system.md) as the normal starting point. The original reference uses approximately twelve weeks before an event. It prioritizes sustainable running speed and running-specific capacity; not routine fasted training or maximizing fat oxidation as an end in itself.

Review the actual previous 3–4 weeks of running frequency, distance, longest outing, symptoms and recovery before increasing load. Low or inconsistent volume calls for tolerance and continuity first; do not raise frequency, length and intensity together just to fit a table. A race next week calls for a realistic effort and less accumulated fatigue, not an emergency capacity-building block.

The twelve-week table is a general programming proposal preserved from the source. It is not an individualized prescription, a guarantee of a target time or an automatically approved app-generated plan. No personal race/date/result/registration or organizer information is included.

## Adapting the rotation

| Core step | Temporary emphasis |
|---|---|
| C1 | Running intervals; controlled threshold-related work earlier, then more race-specific sustained pace near the event. |
| S1 + M | Retain basic strength patterns; simplify/shorten M if it impairs running or recovery. |
| C2 | Easy running, often 45–60 minutes initially; build toward longer outings only with demonstrated tolerance. |
| S2 + M | Retain strength; reduce M/jumps when legs are tired or running load is high. |

Use running for both conditioning steps when tolerated and practical. Cycling/rowing can maintain useful conditioning but do not completely replace running economy and impact tolerance. An optional third easy outing of 20–40 minutes is available only when current running is well tolerated and recovery permits; replace/shorten other work when necessary. It is not a required weekly quota.

## Effort and heart rate

Personal heart-rate zones provide context, not exact physiological thresholds. Pulse lags effort and responds to heat/fatigue. Easy conditioning/recovery has an easy conversational intention; controlled threshold-related efforts and race-specific work require sustainable quality rather than chasing a number. Do not speed up a short hard interval solely to fill a zone.

The source included approximate commercial-zone examples. This migration does not promote them to new fixed phase-zone prescriptions: current official zone defaults remain a product decision, and a commercial HRR zone is not an individually measured lactate threshold. An actual valid measured zone requires the personal profile and appropriate data quality. Missing readings remain unknown. No numerical perceived-effort scale is shown.

## Twelve-week reference progression

The source described inspiration from continuity, controlled threshold work and specificity; it is not a reproduction of an elite athlete's programme. This table retains the generic doses as pragmatic examples. Continue the rotation without fixed weekly session quotas; a row states the emphasis when the relevant conditioning step occurs. Do not compress missed work to catch up.

| Weeks | C1 example | C2 example |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 4 × 5 min controlled hard work, 90 sec easy jog between | 45–55 min easy |
| 3–4 | 3 × 8 min controlled work, 2 min easy jog between | 50–60 min easy |
| 5–6 | 3 × 8 min or 4 × 6 min controlled work, 2 min easy jog between | 55–70 min easy |
| 7–8 | 2 × 10 min near estimated sustainable 10K effort, 3 min jog between | 60–75 min easy |
| 9–10 | 3 × 2 km near 10K pace, 2–3 min jog between; alternatively 3 × 8 min with lower running tolerance | 60–80 min easy |
| 11 | 3 × 5 min near 10K pace; reduce overall volume | 40–55 min easy |
| 12 | Early: 3 × 2 min near 10K pace with ample recovery; later: event | 20–35 min easy if useful |

The source calls for light running preparation/cool-down of approximately 10–15 minutes around C1 sessions. This event-specific reference is not a silent change to Core's approved 4/3 defaults or app Settings; readiness and context matter, and these exact plugin doses still need implementation/individual review before an app plan uses them.

Controlled threshold-related effort is not maximal effort. At a low starting volume, an easy session may remain at 45–60 minutes rather than progressing through the table. Increase only when the previous load is tolerated. Keep S1/S2 but shorten/omit M when needed. Reduce leg fatigue during the last 1–2 weeks and avoid heavy lower-body work to technical failure close to the event. No missed-session repayment.

A recent 5K/10K or comparable training can help estimate an initial race pace. It is an estimate under assumptions about endurance and conditions, not proof of current capacity or a guaranteed future result.

## Food and fluid around the event

The source chose a 10K without energy intake during the race. This is an optional event constraint, not a physiological advantage or a direction to train under-fuelled. For longer efforts, carbohydrate may be useful; individual conditions, tolerance and duration matter. A familiar meal a few hours before racing and adequate normal food beforehand are more practical than a new experiment on race day. Fluid is separate from carbohydrate; plan for conditions and thirst rather than treating no energy intake as no drinking.

Do not routinely train fasted or glycogen-depleted for supposed 10K gains. Extra benefit is uncertain and session quality/recovery may suffer. The [joint nutrition position](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006) provides general sports-nutrition context; the no-fuelling condition is a source design choice, not its universal recommendation. The original [ACSM fluid statement](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31802ca597) is historical context, not a personalized fluid prescription.

## Execution, evaluation and return

Warm up with easy running and a few short progressive efforts appropriate to experience/weather. Start the first 1–2 km controlled, settle into sustainable pace/effort through the middle and increase late only if capacity remains. Heart rate supports interpretation rather than deciding race pace by itself.

Review total time, split consistency, perceived effort in ordinary words, actual running exposure, symptoms and recovery. Compare similar routes/conditions where possible; wearable data is context, not sole authority. After the event return to Core as recovery allows, without compensating for missed steps. Retain only additional habits that remain valuable without the race goal.

The rationale draws on specificity, gradual adaptation, a mix of easier/harder work and reducing fatigue before racing. Those principles do not validate the exact table. The source's taper reference is [Bosquet et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0); its findings are not proof of this particular LOOP taper.
