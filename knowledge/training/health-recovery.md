# LOOP health and recovery

**Translated content draft · supporting guidance**

This resource supports the [LOOP system reference](system.md) with food, sleep, recovery and everyday health. The system owns training, progression and rest. This page adds no mandatory training step or daily compliance checklist. Health matters outside its contribution to the next workout.

## Four everyday principles

1. Eat sufficiently and with variety; support health and training with energy, nutrients and protein.
2. Protect the opportunity for sufficient, regular sleep.
3. Keep moving outside formal training too.
4. Adapt training when sleep, stress, illness or recovery warrants it.

The aim is workable habits over years, not optimization of every day. Family, work, travel and imperfect routines are expected. Food and sleep cannot be replaced by a wearable score, and an imperfect day does not automatically make training pointless.

## Food: enough, nourishing and repeatable

Prioritize the overall pattern: vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains and appropriate unsaturated-fat sources. Persistent under-fuelling can affect health and recovery; harder periods may require more food, not necessarily a deliberate energy surplus. Carbohydrate availability can matter more as demanding endurance work increases.

For someone prioritizing strength and muscle, the source's approximately **1.6–2.2 g protein/kg/day** is a practical planning range, not a universal minimum. Morton et al.'s resistance-training analysis estimated an average breakpoint around 1.6 g/kg/day, with uncertainty extending to roughly 2.2; this does not mean everyone needs the upper value or that benefits rise indefinitely. Whole foods can supply protein; supplementation is not mandatory. See the [original review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5867436/).

No compulsory calorie counting, macro logging, diet phase or compensation for a missed meal is introduced. If recovery/performance declines, consider food alongside sleep, stress, training and possible illness rather than assuming one cause.

## Sleep: protect the opportunity

The source uses **7–9 hours** as a practical adult reference, with individual variation. The AASM/SRS consensus recommends regularly obtaining at least seven hours for healthy adults; it is not a personalized athlete prescription: [adult sleep consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434546/).

Keep reasonably regular sleep opportunities where life allows. Adapt to how you actually feel and perform after a poor night. Repeated insufficient sleep calls for reviewing load and circumstances, not adding another performance obligation around sleep.

## Recovery: judge the whole situation

| Situation | Practical response |
|---|---|
| Ordinary fatigue | Follow Core if movement quality and performance remain reasonable. |
| Clearly reduced recovery | Reduce load, volume or intensity as needed; rest is also available. |
| Illness, injury or marked exhaustion | Prioritize recovery and avoid work that could aggravate the condition. |

A scheduled deload week is not compulsory. Planned or spontaneous unloading remains appropriate when recovery/performance warrants it. Continue the rotation after rest without catching up, following the system owner.

A wearable trend may prompt caution but cannot diagnose the cause or decide alone whether to train. Persistent pain, symptoms or uncertainty about safe exercise need individual assessment. Preserve the habit when appropriate, not at any cost. Fever or concerning systemic symptoms are reasons to avoid demanding training; chest pain, faintness or unusual severe breathlessness require stopping and appropriate medical help. A few days of complete rest is not a failure.

## Everyday health without another control system

- **Movement:** limit prolonged sitting and move regularly. The system owns the practical step reference; it is not a medical threshold.
- **Alcohol:** less is generally better for health; consider sleep/recovery too. No moral label or compensation workout is required.
- **Stress:** recovery includes work/family demands, social connection, enjoyable activities and realistic expectations. No specific daily ritual is mandatory.
- **Breathing/down-regulation:** calm breathing may feel useful. It does not automatically demonstrate restored autonomic balance.
- **Load:** continuity is valuable but does not automatically prevent injuries. Technique, capacity, adaptation and recovery still matter.

## Supplements: a need before a routine

No supplement is compulsory. Consider plausible benefit, risk, cost and simplicity, and individual medical circumstances.

| Supplement | Source position retained as optional guidance |
|---|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | Optional for strength/repeated high-intensity work; 3–5 g/day is a commonly used maintenance amount for healthy adults. See the [ISSN position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/); not a requirement for LOOP. |
| Vitamin D | Consider deficiency or inadequate intake; individual need/dose is not determined by this training system. |
| Omega-3 | Prioritize suitable dietary sources such as fish; capsules are not a universal cardiovascular-health requirement. |
| Caffeine | Optional performance aid; weigh sleep effects, tolerance and unwanted effects. |

Medical conditions, medicines, pregnancy or uncertainty call for individual advice rather than a generic supplement instruction.
