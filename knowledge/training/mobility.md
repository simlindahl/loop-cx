# LOOP mobility resource

**Translated content draft · optional supporting resource**

This resource complements the [LOOP system reference](system.md). It is a short option for useful range, less perceived stiffness and calm movement. It does not add a mandatory daily routine to Core. The original document suggested daily practice; that is an option when useful, not a rule overriding the system's needs-based mobility approach.

## Basic routine

Use comfortable breaths rather than a stopwatch:

| Movement | Example amount | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Cat–cow | 6 breaths | Gentle spinal movement |
| World's greatest stretch | 5 breaths per side | Hips and thoracic movement |
| 90/90 hip switches | 8 breaths | Active hip rotation |
| Deep squat hold | 8 breaths | Hips and ankles |
| Shoulder rolls and arm circles | 6 breaths | Shoulder movement and relaxation |

Breathe calmly without forcing depth or holding the breath. Spread dynamic movements over the breaths; in static positions, stay comfortable and breathe normally. Use controlled, pain-free range and develop control gradually. There is no requirement to force an end position or turn the routine into a performance test.

For a concrete limitation, optionally add a targeted movement such as a standing calf stretch or prone chest opener. Return to the simpler routine when the addition no longer serves a need. Omit or adapt any movement that is inappropriate for the individual.

The routine may feel restorative. Do not promise that it speeds physiological recovery after training. A review found no convincing advantage of post-exercise stretching over passive recovery for strength or delayed soreness; the evidence has limitations and does not directly test this routine: [Afonso et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8133317/).
