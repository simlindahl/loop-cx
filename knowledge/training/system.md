# LOOP system reference

**Content and ownership migration draft · LOOP by Simon Lindahl**

Built for interrupted lives; intended to develop lasting physical capability. This draft translates and reconciles the original Swedish system text. It proposes this file as the single owner of general LOOP training rules. The [What is LOOP? guide](../what-is-loop.md) explains these rules; it does not independently prescribe another programme. App recording, delivery and interaction rules remain with their existing product specifications. This migration does not enable the reader or implement S/M, training plugins or Ghost.

## Purpose and assumptions

LOOP starts with an explicit assumption: **life will interrupt training**. Work, family, travel and illness are conditions the system must tolerate, rather than evidence that the user has failed. A sequence is easier to resume than a calendar that accumulates missed sessions.

The intended user already has training experience, can choose suitable exercises and can judge movement quality and recovery. This is a design assumption, not a claim that every experienced person is ready for every dose. Unfamiliar movements and returning after a layoff require practice and rebuilding tolerance. The system cannot assess an injury or medical condition.

The aim is broad capability: strength across basic movement patterns, aerobic capacity, usable movement and work under mixed fatigue. LOOP trades some specialization and programming precision for low decision cost and continuity. It is not a peaking plan or a guarantee of health, injury prevention, obstacle-race readiness or performance. A particular sport may need skills, impact tolerance, volume or speed practice that Core does not supply.

Minimalism means enough useful training with few decisions, not the shortest possible session. Add a component only for an important unmet need; first try replacing, simplifying or adapting what exists. Accept smaller gaps instead of separately optimizing every ability. Pareto is a prioritization heuristic here, not evidence that a particular dose delivers 80 percent of possible results.

## Stimuli and rotation

| Element | Intended contribution | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| C · Conditioning | Aerobic endurance and the capacity for harder work | Easy volume and interval formats emphasize different demands; none replaces sport-specific practice. |
| S · Strength | Force production and control across basic movement patterns | The deliberately small default is not maximal strength or hypertrophy programming. |
| M · Mixed | Locomotion, grip, carrying and simple work under combined fatigue | It complements rather than replaces dedicated strength and conditioning. |

Energy systems act together; these are programming emphases, not separate physiological compartments.

**C1 → S1 + M → C2 → S2 + M → repeat.**

M follows strength; it is not a fifth rotation step. There are no fixed rest days or weekly quotas. Rest also when recovery requires it, rather than assuming life will always provide enough rest. Normally continue with the next step after an interruption. Do not compensate for missed sessions. Restarting at C1 can be practical, but frequent restarts can change the actual balance of stimuli.

Order is not dose. A rotation completed over fourteen days gives less exposure per week than the same rotation completed over seven. Evaluate actual sessions, everyday activity and recovery over time. WHO's adult activity recommendations give external health context, not a new LOOP quota or proof that any completed rotation supplies enough activity: [WHO physical activity guidance](https://www.who.int/initiatives/behealthy/physical-activity).

## Conditioning

Choose a simple familiar modality, such as running, rowing, cycling or an air bike. Core permits these alternatives; the current running app does not thereby acquire complete modes for them. Conditioning should not be limited primarily by a technically difficult lift or local muscular failure.

<a id="c1-one-interval-option-per-c1-step"></a>
### C1 · One interval option per C1 step

The later owner-approved protocols replace the original single 5 × 3-minute template and its 10/5-minute preparation. The original instruction to repeat all four efforts was inconsistent with its five-effort table and is not carried forward.

| Official variant | Purpose | Default main set | Main / total with default 4/3 minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1.1 · Sprint intervals (SIT) | Repeated maximal, all-out sprints with substantial easy recovery | 7 × 30 seconds work; 6 × 3 minutes recovery | 21:30 / 28:30 |
| C1.2 · Aerobic intervals (HIIT) | Sustained, controlled hard efforts for aerobic-power practice | 5 × 3 minutes work; 4 × 2 minutes recovery | 23 / 30 minutes |
| C1.3 · Threshold intervals | Controlled, sustained effort near the individual's threshold; sustainable pacing takes priority | 3 × 6 minutes work; 2 × 3 minutes recovery | 24 / 31 minutes |

These are alternatives, not three consecutive Core steps. The selected repetition count may vary while main-set time, including recovery, remains 10–60 minutes: SIT 4–18, HIIT 3–12, threshold 2–7. There is no recovery after the final work effort. These bounds are product guardrails, not validation of every possible dose.

Shared default warm-up/cool-down is 4/3 minutes. Current global app Settings can independently resolve either phase to Off or 1–15 whole minutes for new C1 starts. Started and historical runs keep the resolved plan. Runtime extension/skip remains available. A timer reaching zero does not prove readiness; particularly before maximal sprinting, prepare sufficiently and use suitable conditions. Preparation is not a universal safety guarantee.

Choose controlled repeatable effort for HIIT/threshold and preserve the maximal intent of SIT without making SIT an automatic progression. Pulse responds with a delay: do not accelerate a short sprint just to chase a low reading. High-zone minutes are not a pass/fail score, proof of time at maximal oxygen uptake or a reason to extend a session. The threshold option can serve its purpose with fewer high-zone minutes.

The owner has removed numerical perceived-effort ratings from presentation. This does not convert them to heart-rate zones or change historical data. Exact official phase-zone defaults and the future place of optional pace targets remain separate product decisions. Until decided, this reference supplies no new numerical zone prescription. A programme's intended zone is not the same as a measured zone; missing sensor/profile data stays unknown. [Running-workout evidence](../running-workouts.md#research-basis-and-transfer-limits) owns the app's research mapping and measurement limits.

### C2 · Easy, steady conditioning

Default duration is 45 minutes; the approved personal duration range is 20–120 minutes in five-minute steps. It has no separate warm-up/cool-down phases. These later approved defaults supersede the older generic 45–60-minute wording without changing old recordings.

Zone 2 describes the intended easy aerobic emphasis. Comfortable conversation is a useful practical cue, not an exact calculation of personal zones or a laboratory threshold. Use a sustainable modality and adapt to conditions and tolerance. Do not force a previous pace when heat, fatigue or symptoms make it inappropriate. No wearable is required to participate; the app must not invent a measured zone when data is absent. The [CDC intensity explanation](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html) gives general context for the talk test.

## Strength: S1 and S2

### Exercise order and exposure

Use the same order in both working rounds:

- **S1:** RDL → horizontal pull → pistol-squat progression → horizontal push.
- **S2:** pistol-squat progression → vertical pull → RDL → vertical push.

Both include the hinge and single-leg squat so each pattern is exposed twice per completed rotation. Order gives S1 a hinge priority and S2 a squat priority without adding sets. Horizontal/vertical pulling and pressing complement each other.

Pistol squat is a deliberate compromise: unilateral strength, balance, control and active range are valued even when these limit loading before the leg muscles do. It does not claim superiority to a heavy bilateral squat for maximal knee-dominant strength. Use support, a stable box or a suitable progression through a controlled, pain-free range. Add difficulty/load only when execution is consistent.

RDL supplies a progressively loadable hip hinge for the posterior chain without requiring a floor deadlift. Conventional deadlifting remains a useful skill, but is not a standard requirement. Choose equipment and regressions that allow safe control. A home setup needs a genuinely safe pulling/anchoring option; acknowledge a missing pattern instead of claiming any substitute is identical.

### Sets, rest and technical failure

Perform one working set of each exercise in order, for two rounds: **two working sets per exercise**, and two per leg for the pistol progression. General warm-up, jumps when appropriate and ramp-up sets precede the circuit and do not count as working sets.

Technical failure means another complete repetition would not meet the intended range, posture and stability. Stop at the last clean repetition; do not deliberately provoke a failed repetition under a load or continue after loss of position. Safety overrides the failure default. With unfamiliar movements or impaired recovery, practice, reduce load/dose, leave clean repetitions available or rest as needed.

About 60 seconds between working sets is a starting point, not a ceiling. Extend rest to preserve the next set's quality, especially after hard work. Allow roughly 15–30 seconds or longer between sides as needed. This is not a strength circuit performed against a conditioning clock.

Two hard sets are a simplicity/effort tradeoff, not an assertion that low volume or failure is optimal for every person. Fatigue and technique can limit safe stimulus; more demanding strength/hypertrophy goals may need different programming. The [ACSM 2026 position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) supports adaptable resistance training rather than a universal requirement to train to failure. It does not validate LOOP's exact combination.

### Slow, quality-led progression

These rules apply to S1/S2 strength work, not M or jumps:

- Bodyweight movements: increase difficulty or add load when both working sets reach 20 clean repetitions at technical failure.
- Loaded movements: use a pragmatic 6–10-repetition range. Increase load by a small available step when both sets reach the upper part, roughly 8–10 clean repetitions, with comparable technique and technical failure.
- For pistol progressions, support and difficulty may need adjustment before added load. A shorter range is not automatically progress.

Progress when the same work becomes easier with the same quality. Missed sessions alone do not demand a deload, but persistent fatigue, declining performance or symptoms can justify less training. Do not automatically add strength sets to solve a recovery problem.

## M · Short mixed finish after S1 and S2

Normally use 10–15 minutes after each strength session. Strength comes first to protect its quality. M's conditioning purpose takes priority: combine simple locomotion/cyclical work with manageable resistance, not heavy technical lifting or local failure under fatigue.

An OCR-inspired example, repeated in controlled flow within the time available:

1. Run or use suitable locomotion for about 1–2 minutes.
2. Farmer carry for 40–60 metres with a controllable load.
3. Optionally perform up to five controlled jump squats while landing quality remains good; otherwise omit them.
4. Dead hang for 30–45 seconds without grip failure; shorten or support the feet as needed.

Not every component must fit every session. Adapt space/equipment and reduce or omit M when strength, recovery or symptoms warrant it. Jumps while fatigued do not replace quality jumps in the warm-up. These doses are pragmatic examples, not experimentally established optima.

Placing M after strength reduces separate-session administration, but does not make fatigue or interference disappear. Concurrent aerobic/strength training can be compatible with maximal strength and muscle size, while explosive-strength adaptation may be more vulnerable, particularly within the same session. That evidence concerns other programmes, not this exact short finisher: [Schumann et al., 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7). The tradeoff is deliberate; adjust M before insisting that every component be completed regardless of recovery.

## Preparation, explosive movement and mobility

Warm up with light movement, rehearse the day's patterns and ramp load before demanding strength work. The aim is readiness for the main stimulus, not a long generic ritual. Add time when necessary rather than rushing heavier ramp-up sets.

When suitable, integrate **3 × 3 standing long jumps in S1** and **3 × 3 vertical jumps in S2**, after becoming warm and before loaded working sets, with generous rest. Start with simpler submaximal versions/lower volume if unfamiliar. Stop or omit when landing quality deteriorates, pain appears, fatigue is unusual or the environment is unsuitable. This is a chosen exposure, not a validated minimum effective dose. Separate accelerations are not a standard Core addition; sport-specific speed can belong to a temporary adaptation.

Use controlled individually appropriate range in normal exercises. Target mobility only for a meaningful limitation; optional 20–30-second single-leg balance per side can fit the existing warm-up if needed. The [mobility routine](mobility.md) is an optional resource, not a new mandatory daily task. Light movement or calm breathing after training may feel helpful without establishing a special physiological reset.

On ordinary days aim for at least 7,000 steps, adapted for illness, injury and circumstances. This is a habit reference, not a medical threshold or repayment for a missed workout. [Health and recovery](health-recovery.md) owns supporting lifestyle guidance without adding mandatory training steps.

## Optional competition and Ghost

A voluntary race, familiar-route comparison or self-competition can give practice a concrete purpose, feedback and variety. LOOP includes room for mastery and enjoyment, not only completion of a minimum dose. These are design reasons for offering competition, not a promise that competition improves everybody's motivation or health.

Self-reference makes the challenge personally meaningful: practise a familiar route, judge execution against your own experience and notice mastery without ranking people with different training histories, opportunities or conditions. In Ghost, a first valid result can establish a baseline; later starts use the permitted year-best or personal-best reference under the frozen route/version rules, not an arbitrary target or any selected old attempt. A baseline is not a third selectable ghost type. Choosing personal reference over a public leaderboard is LOOP's design choice, not a proven universal ranking of motivational methods. Keep the choice voluntary and interpret conditions honestly. Health value comes from sustainable participation, not from continually breaking records.

Ghost is a separate optional race experience. It is not another Core step, an extra effort target for C2, a health score or an instruction to race every easy day. Race execution has its own route/reference/quality rules in the [Ghost specification](../ghost-run.md); it does not inherit C1/C2 effort targets. A completed continuous run may supply a route without making the later race a Core workout.

A controlled study in 25 young adults found greater enjoyment for self-competition than visual feedback during short VR cycling bouts. This is a different activity, device and timescale; it does not establish outdoor Ghost-running benefits or long-term adherence. See the [original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11548122/). The wider rationale remains a hypothesis to evaluate in actual use, not invented LOOP user research.

Comparison can also create pressure, excess intensity or discouragement. A slower result may reflect weather, terrain, recovery or GPS/reference quality rather than lost fitness. Opting out, ending an attempt or doing an easy session must remain legitimate. No compulsory race frequency, automatic progression, rotation change or compensation rule is introduced here. Keep competition subordinate to the intended training/recovery, not the reverse.

## Temporary event adaptations

Core is the default. An imminent event first calls for realistic assessment of running/hurdle experience, route demands and recovery, not a sudden attempt to build missing capacity. OCR is a useful illustration of broad demands, not a readiness guarantee: running impact tolerance and actual obstacle skill require specific exposure.

With roughly 4–8 weeks available, an OCR adaptation can temporarily emphasize terrain, grip, carrying and obstacle practice. Replace or adapt existing work before adding load. It may adjust content/order/frequency while protecting recovery and retaining useful strength/aerobic exposure. Return to Core afterward without catching up. Gym movements are not full substitutes for obstacle technique; general capacity also does not replace acceleration, braking and direction changes in ball sports.

The [generic 10K adaptation](plugins/10k.md) preserves the original optional twelve-week reference. It is not a personal race plan, a new app implementation or a claim that its exact doses fit every user.

## Evidence and provenance

LOOP's complete rotation, exercise selection, set counts, jump dose, preparation and M format have not been validated as a complete intervention. Evidence for component training methods informs a pragmatic design; it does not establish an optimal programme or eliminate risk. Physiological thresholds, commercial zones, talk cues and measured heart rate are related information, not interchangeable measurements.
