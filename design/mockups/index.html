What is LOOP?

Training that fits a changing life.

You know how to train. Finding room for it is the harder part now.

LOOP gives conditioning, strength and mixed movement a simple repeating order. Do the next useful session when life allows.

C1 → S1 + M
→ C2 → S2 + M

No perfect week required. No app or wearable needed.

Get started

Four steps. Read at your own pace.

A practical guide · no setup required

1. Understand the rotation

C1 → S1 + M
→ C2 → S2 + M

C is conditioning. S is strength. M is a mixed finish after strength. Rest when needed; continue with the next step after an interruption.

2. Choose exercises and load

Use familiar movements and safe equipment. Gym and home versions should preserve the movement's purpose.

Start loaded exercises around 6–10 clean repetitions. Core uses two working sets per exercise to technical failure: stop before the next repetition would lose range, posture or control.

This is a LOOP default, not a requirement for all training results.

3. Complete your first session

Starting fresh and ready for hard conditioning? C1 is 10 minutes warm-up, 5 × 3 minutes controlled hard effort, 2 minutes easy between efforts and 5 minutes easy to finish. Total: 38 minutes.

Returning after a layoff? Rebuild tolerance before treating the full dose as a target.

Read all Core workout cards→

4. Continue and adapt

Record what you did and note the next step. Do not catch up missed sessions.

Progress loaded movements when both sets reach roughly 8–10 clean reps; bodyweight movements at 20. Increase difficulty modestly.

On a demanding day, rest or reduce the dose. A shorter session can preserve continuity without being equivalent to the full session.

Ready for the next useful session

Know your next step. Choose a safe version. Leave room to recover.

S1 · Strength + mixed

Two rounds. Clean repetitions.

Warm up, then rehearse with lighter loads. Optional standing long jumps: 3 × 3 before strength, only with controlled landings.

  1. Romanian deadlift
  2. Horizontal pull
  3. Supported pistol progression, each leg
  4. Horizontal push

One working set per exercise per round. Two sets in total, two per leg for the squat. Start around 60 seconds rest; extend when needed for quality.

At home

Dumbbell or backpack RDL. Secure band row. Supported pistol to a stable box. Push-up at a suitable height.

A safe pulling anchor is required. Follow the equipment's rating and fitting instructions.

Stop at technical failure

End at the last clean rep, before the next would lose range, posture or control. Pain, dizziness or lost balance means stop.

Finish with M

10–15 minutes of manageable mixed movement. Shorten or omit when fatigue calls for it.

Understand the choices. Keep it useful.

Enough training for what?

LOOP aims at general capability, not a promised race result. Actual training dose matters: a rotation completed in two weeks is different from one completed in one week.

Strength without extra decisions

Two working sets to technical failure are the Core default. Research does not make failure compulsory or two sets optimal for everyone.

Recovery is more than a score

Protect sleep, eat enough and keep ordinary daily movement. Stress and illness matter too. A wearable is context, not a command.

Rest, lower the load, reduce sets, shorten conditioning or omit M when needed. Do not repay training debt.

Mobility work can address a need; no separate daily routine or supplement is required.
